\section{Related work}\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Heuristics for the CVRP.}
Variable neighborhood search systematically changes neighborhoods during descent and perturbation \citep{mladenovic1997,hansen2001}. For the CVRP, the strongest published heuristics are population-based or large-scale specialized methods; the hybrid genetic search of \citet{vidal2022}, with its SWAP* neighborhood, and the scalable local search of \citet{accorsi2021} define the current quality level, evaluated on the benchmark of \citet{uchoa2017} among others. We use the former only as a reference ceiling (Section~\ref{sec:ceiling}); the method we study is a lightweight VNS whose appeal is simplicity rather than absolute quality.

\paragraph{Adaptive operator selection.}
Choosing which operator to apply from observed rewards is a multi-armed-bandit problem \citep{auer2002,suttonbarto2018}. In evolutionary computation, adaptive pursuit and probability matching \citep{thierens2005}, dynamic bandits \citep{dacosta2008} and a systematic analysis of bandit-based mechanisms \citep{fialho2010} established the framework; adaptive weights are also central to adaptive large neighborhood search \citep{ropke2006}, whose adaptive layer a meta-analysis quantified as a small average gain \citep{turkes2021}. Hyper-heuristics generalize the idea to selecting among low-level heuristics \citep{burke2013}; recent work includes bandit-based hyper-heuristics \citep{lagos2024}, Q-learning for operator selection in iterated greedy \citep{karimimamaghan2023}, correlation-assisted operator selection \citep{pei2023}, and a ranking of operators for routing neighborhoods \citep{voigt2025}. Surveys of machine learning inside metaheuristics are given by \citet{karimimamaghan2022} and \citet{bengio2021}. Our study differs in its object (a specific published CVRP method), in its controls (matched non-adaptive baselines, a tuned static mixture, a factorial decomposition of the surrounding search) and in its inference (equivalence tests, not only significance tests).

\paragraph{Learning for routing.}
Neural constructive and improvement methods learn policies for routing end to end \citep{nazari2018,kool2019,kwon2020,hottung2020}, and automatic algorithm design with large language models has recently been applied to combinatorial problems \citep{romera2024,liu2024eoh}. These approaches differ from operator-selection bandits in what is learned and when; we include one pretrained neural construction method only as zero-shot context.

\paragraph{Experimental methodology.}
That heuristic comparisons require matched budgets, honest baselines and careful reporting has been argued for decades \citep{hooker1995,barr1995}, and the proliferation of novelty without generalizable findings has been criticized \citep{sorensen2015}. For comparing algorithms over a set of instances we use paired nonparametric tests with multiplicity correction \citep{wilcoxon1945,holm1979,derrac2011}; to support claims of \emph{no difference} we use equivalence testing \citep{lakens2017}; and for hyperparameter tuning we follow the principle of one protocol for all compared methods, as in automatic configuration \citep{lopezibanez2016}.

\paragraph{Gap addressed.}
To our knowledge, no study has isolated the learning component of a bandit-guided CVRP heuristic from its search structure under matched budgets, with all policies tuned by the same protocol and with equivalence testing. We do so for HG-UCB \citep{kalatzantonakis2023}, whose reproduction we report in full.
