\section{Related work}\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Heuristics for the CVRP}
Variable neighborhood search systematically changes neighborhoods during descent and perturbation \citep{mladenovic1997,hansen2001}. For the CVRP, the strongest published heuristics are population-based or large-scale specialized methods; the hybrid genetic search of \citet{vidal2022}, with its SWAP* neighborhood, and the scalable local search of \citet{accorsi2021} define the current quality level, evaluated on the benchmark of \citet{uchoa2017} among others. We use the former only as a reference ceiling (Section~\ssec{sec:ceiling}); the method we study is a lightweight VNS whose appeal is simplicity rather than absolute quality.

\paragraph{Adaptive operator selection}
Choosing which operator to apply from observed rewards is a multi-armed-bandit problem \citep{auer2002,suttonbarto2018}. In evolutionary computation, adaptive pursuit and probability matching \citep{thierens2005}, dynamic bandits \citep{dacosta2008} and a systematic analysis of bandit-based mechanisms \citep{fialho2010} established the framework; adaptive weights are also central to adaptive large neighborhood search \citep{ropke2006}, whose adaptive layer a meta-analysis quantified as a small average gain \citep{turkes2021}. Hyper-heuristics generalize the idea to selecting among low-level heuristics \citep{burke2013}; recent work includes bandit-based hyper-heuristics \citep{lagos2024}, Q-learning for operator selection in iterated greedy \citep{karimimamaghan2023}, correlation-assisted operator selection \citep{pei2023}, and a ranking of operators for routing neighborhoods \citep{voigt2025}. Neural methods that learn routing policies end to end \citep{kool2019} differ from operator-selection bandits in what is learned and when. These mechanisms are typically studied on operators, such as mutations, whose usefulness changes along the run; the best-improvement local-search operators of a VNS are deterministic and form a different setting, which is part of what we examine.

\paragraph{Other problems} The same questions arise for the traveling salesman problem, where the Lin--Kernighan family sets the quality level \citep{helsgaun2000} on the standard library of instances \citep{reinelt1991}, and for the CVRP with time windows, where benchmarks with proven optima exist \citep{solomon1987} and modern solvers handle time windows with constant-time segment checks \citep{vidal2013,wouda2024}; we use them as references and as the setting of three generality studies.

\paragraph{Experimental methodology}
Matched budgets and honest baselines have been argued for decades \citep{hooker1995,barr1995}, and novelty without generalizable findings has been criticized \citep{sorensen2015}. We compare algorithms over instance sets with paired nonparametric tests and multiplicity correction \citep{wilcoxon1945,holm1979,derrac2011}, support claims of \emph{no difference} with equivalence testing \citep{lakens2017}, and tune all methods by one protocol \citep{lopezibanez2016}.

\paragraph{Gap addressed}
To our knowledge, no study has isolated the learning component of a bandit-guided CVRP heuristic from its search structure under matched budgets, with matched non-adaptive baselines, a tuned static mixture, one tuning protocol for all policies and equivalence testing. We do so for HG-UCB \citep{kalatzantonakis2023}, whose reproduction we report.
